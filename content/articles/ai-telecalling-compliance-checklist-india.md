# AI Telecalling Compliance Checklist for India

AI calling is not one legal category. The risk depends on who initiated the conversation, the purpose, the route, the data used, the industry and the action the system can take. Treat this checklist as an engineering and procurement starting point, not legal advice.

## Before a call

Record the lawful business purpose, source of the number, consent or customer request, caller identity, approved script boundaries, language, quiet hours and opt-out behavior. Confirm the telecom provider's role, number ownership, route and complaint process. For sensitive sectors, identify the human owner who approves the workflow and the data-retention period.

## During the call

The agent should identify itself accurately, avoid invented promises, stop when the customer asks, transfer on low confidence or a sensitive request and avoid collecting information that the workflow does not need. Recording notices, transcript access and redaction should be designed before launch. A human should be reachable when the caller disputes an account, asks for a regulated decision or cannot understand the system.

## After the call

Persist disposition, consent/opt-out change, transfer reason and tool actions. Restrict recordings and transcripts by role. Test deletion, export, incident response and repeated opt-out. Review a sample of calls across Indian English, Hindi and code-mixed speech rather than trusting a single demo.

Vaiket's [AI telecalling research](/posts/ai-telecalling-in-india) explains the technical layers. Public Vaiket docs do not independently establish every voice route or regulatory status, so confirm current availability in writing.
