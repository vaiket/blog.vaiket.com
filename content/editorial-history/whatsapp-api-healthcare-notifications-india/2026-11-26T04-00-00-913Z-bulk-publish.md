# WhatsApp API Healthcare Notifications in India: Consent, Privacy and Human Escalation

*Research snapshot: 26 November 2026.*

**Editorial disclosure:** Healthcare workflows need sector-specific legal, clinical and security review. This article is an implementation framework, not medical or legal advice.

## Minimise the message

An appointment reminder usually needs a patient-controlled identifier, date, time, location and contact route. It does not automatically need diagnosis, prescription, test result or detailed clinical history. Keep sensitive context behind an authenticated portal or trained human team.

Meta's [WhatsApp Business Platform documentation](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/overview) covers messaging, templates and webhooks. The healthcare operator must add its own consent, identity, access, retention and escalation controls. A provider's dashboard does not replace clinical governance.

## Workflow controls

- Capture purpose-specific consent and preferred contact channel.
- Validate the patient identifier before selecting a record.
- Use templates with minimum necessary content.
- Provide a human route for rescheduling, complaints and urgent questions.
- Never use an automated message as emergency triage without an approved clinical process.
- Log access, send decision, opt-out and deletion events.

## Test cases

Test a shared family phone, wrong number, language preference, missed appointment, reply asking for medical advice, opt-out, message sent outside working hours and a webhook replay. The expected outcome should be written before the pilot.

## Data and access controls

Keep scheduling identifiers separate from clinical notes. Use role-based access for support and operations, record every manual lookup, and define retention for outbound messages, delivery events and attachments. A webhook endpoint should validate authenticity, reject replayed events and avoid logging full message bodies by default. Test whether an operator can see only the facilities and queues assigned to them.

Include a patient who shares a family number, a caregiver who is authorised but not the patient, a wrong-number correction, a changed appointment, duplicate scheduler events, a language change and a patient asking a clinical question. The safe response for medical uncertainty is an authorised human or approved care route.

The pilot should demonstrate no duplicate notification for one appointment event, a deterministic reschedule path, an accessible preference change, an auditable human handoff and a reconciliation report for failed reminders. These are operational controls, not a claim of clinical benefit.
